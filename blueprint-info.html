Why It Matters

Independence is rarely lost all at once. It erodes.

Most people don't lose their independence to one bad fall. They lose it slowly. Strength fades, balance declines, and then one ordinary day the body can't do an ordinary thing.

The walker shows up, then the wheelchair, then the room you stop leaving. None of it is random — it's the slow erosion of strength, balance, and mobility.

Here's the part most people miss: the capacities that decide how well you move at 80 are built — or lost — in your 30s, 40s, and 50s.

How fast you walk. The strength in your legs. Your grip. Whether you can stand on one foot. They're not fixed, and they're not luck. They're trainable, starting now.
